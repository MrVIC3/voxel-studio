# Architecture and craft

## Coherent composition

Begin with a recognizable silhouette, a main mass and subordinate masses. A roof, tower or hall should establish the hierarchy; minor gables, porches and outbuildings should support it. Size the structure for a player: an entrance should read clearly from the approach, circulation generally needs two blocks of headroom, and large rooms need intentional floor/ceiling structure rather than a giant hollow shell. An odd/even footprint decision affects ridge, windows and door centering: choose it early.

On slopes, step the foundation and paths into the terrain; do not leave hovering corners or bury windows. Bridges need coherent landings and supports. Give plazas edges, routes, negative space and destinations. For settlements, establish street hierarchy, roof heights, shared material language and distinct building purposes before individual decoration.

Assign palette roles such as foundation, primary wall, structural frame, roof, trim, glazing, metal and vegetation. Distinguish structure from infill using depth and construction logic, not only contrasting colors. Start with a small coherent set; add materials only when they earn a role. Color and value should let the main form read at a distance.

## Intentional detail

Give wall planes depth through real bays, reveals, posts, eaves and setbacks. Repeat a legible rhythm, then interrupt it at the entrance, stair, corner or important room. Roofs need thickness, overhangs and plausible junctions: every ridge, valley, dormer, verge and chimney penetration should be checked from above and from the rear. Avoid flat walls decorated with unrelated slabs, trapdoors, leaves and lanterns.

Use stairs and slabs to articulate roof pitches, cornices and masonry transitions. Check facing and shape; a stair's `facing` points toward its high side. Doors require upper and lower halves. Glass panes/fences/walls require explicit side connections because strict bridge placement does not compute neighbors. Leaf blocks used in custom trees generally need `persistent=true`. Give custom trees a trunk/branch structure and grouped foliage volumes with a deliberate outline.

Build interiors around use: routes first, furniture groups second, storage/work/light last. Make every floor reachable, keep door swing and stair headroom clear, and align exterior openings with rooms. Use floor materials and beams to clarify space. Functional chests, signs, beds and other block-entity blocks cannot currently be placed by this bridge; do not fake support for them in the completion report.

Surface variation should follow causes. Moss collects where moisture persists, heavier stone carries loads, worn routes connect destinations, and weathering is locally coherent. A deterministic hash sprinkling unrelated blocks everywhere still looks random. Prefer hand-placed clusters and line/range operations with a stated material role.

## Review criteria

Judge the build at three distances: silhouette/composition, facade rhythm/material depth, and player-scale joins/details. Review the rear and service sides with the same care as the front. Check corners, repeated module end caps, seam intersections, supports, lighting placement and the landscape edge. Seek coherence and intentional variation; symmetry is useful where it supports the brief, not a universal goal.

For a complex build, make a small defect ledger: location, visible symptom, intended correction, verification view. Fix the highest-impact form and circulation problems before ornament. Describe remaining issues candidly. No score, block count, palette size or automated occupancy check can certify professional design quality; actual visual judgment remains essential.

## References and sculpture

Translate a supplied reference into a short hierarchy before geometry: what must read from the approach, what gives the middle distance its rhythm, and which close details identify its style. Compare the actual render to those priorities. A reference image is not a block-accurate plan; resolve impossible thinness or lighting through deliberate Minecraft-scale choices.

For dragons, figures and organic monuments, settle pose, gesture and silhouette before scales or spikes. Model connected torso/neck/head masses and intentional joints, then wings, limbs and tail. Check the face from the approach and both sides; eyes, brows, jaw opening and horn direction carry more identity than surface noise. Give wing membranes coherent ribs and attached scalloped borders, and taper appendages rather than ending in disconnected cubes. Use the connected tube helper and connectivity diagnostics before expensive texture work.

Judge the build at player eye level as well as in flattering aerial views. Keep a quiet material field around focal details, control bright accents so they stay dominant, and correct junctions/end caps before adding more ornament. Preserve a user-approved face, palette or silhouette during unrelated repairs; pin its precise region when useful.
